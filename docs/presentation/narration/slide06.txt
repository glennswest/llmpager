Before any of this could happen, the hardware fought back. Brand new motherboard, first boot, and the GPU simply vanished. A power management bug wedged it so hard it fell off the PCI bus entirely, and no amount of resetting would bring it back. Two host reboots. A dive through kernel logs. One obscure vfio parameter buried in a forum thread. And then, twenty nine minutes after it died, the card came back to life. Line one of actual code hadn't even been written yet. Once it was alive, we measured the machine, and those four numbers on the right became the speed limits that shaped every design decision. GPU memory: four hundred forty eight gigabytes a second. The PCIe link: twenty five. The disk: four point three. A motorway, a main road, and a side street. The entire design is about keeping traffic off the side street.
