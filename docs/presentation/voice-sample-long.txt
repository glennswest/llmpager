Hi, I'm Glenn West, and I want to tell you about a project called llmpager.
The idea is simple to say and tricky to build: run a language model that is
bigger than the graphics card it lives on. The model has thirty billion
parameters, about eighteen and a half gigabytes of weights, and the card
only holds sixteen. Most people would say that can't work. But the model
only uses a small piece of itself for every word it writes, and that
changes everything.

Think of a desk and a filing cabinet. The desk is fast and small. The
cabinet is slow and huge. If you keep the folders you use most on the
desk, and fetch the rare ones only when you need them, you almost never
wait. That is the whole trick. The cache holds forty-eight experts per
layer, the disk moves four gigabytes per second, and every number in this
talk was measured, not guessed.

We got our first real words out of the machine three hours in. The prompt
was: the capital of France is. And the model wrote: Paris. The capital of
the United Kingdom is London. Watching that happen, with the weights
streaming off the disk in real time, was the moment this stopped being a
theory and became an engine.
